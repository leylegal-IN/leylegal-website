How it works

Inside Ley Legal

Five layers under every answer.

  1. IPC, CrPC and the Evidence Act beside BNS, BNSS and BSA. The old-to-new tables are checked by a legal reviewer, not written by AI.

  2. Supreme Court and High Court judgments from open data are searched first. Indian Kanoon fills the gaps. Nothing comes from the open web.

  3. Stage, next date and orders for any case, by CNR, case number or party name.

  4. Ask in English, Hindi or Hinglish, the way you would ask a colleague. It works out the legal terms.

  5. Every judgment is checked against the database before you see it. If nothing fits, it says “not sure”.

Three examples of Ley Legal at work

  1. Research. Asked “498A mein seedha arrest ho sakta hai?”, it answers that arrest is not automatic under Section 35 of the BNSS, gives the provision (Section 85, BNS, formerly Section 498A, IPC) and cites Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar, (2014) 8 SCC 273, with the citation checked.
  2. Case status. Given a CNR number, it shows the court, the parties, the stage, the last order and the next hearing date.
  3. Sections. Asked in Hindi for the new section for IPC 302, it gives Section 103(1), BNS, gives the definition in Section 101, BNS, and warns that Section 302 of the BNS is a different offence.
